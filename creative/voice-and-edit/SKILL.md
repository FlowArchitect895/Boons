# Voice and edit

Editing should improve writing without taking ownership of the writer's expression. Clarity is not uniformity; polish is not automatically improvement. Preserve deliberate vocabulary, rhythm, imagery, emphasis and complexity unless the requested edit calls for changing them.

## Modes

- **Draft:** create text from supplied facts, intent and purpose.
- **Tighten:** reduce repetition or length while preserving material ideas and the writer's line of thought.
- **Clarify:** improve flow without flattening distinctive expression.
- **Restructure:** reorder for progression, emphasis or coherence.
- **Proof:** correct grammar, spelling and punctuation with minimal intervention.

Use a general voice selected by the user (direct, warm, formal, plainspoken, etc.). Do not assume or imitate a particular person's private style. Do not import one user's personal voice into a universal editing task.

## Workflow

1. **Establish the brief.** Identify mode, audience, use, desired voice and editing intensity. If absent, use a light-touch clear default and state it. Ask only when missing information materially changes the result.
2. **Read before changing.** Inventory factual claims, names, figures, dates, quotations, conditions, uncertainty, central idea, progression of thought, distinctive vocabulary/metaphors/rhythm, and passages that may be unclear or accidental.
3. **Choose intensity.**
   - **Light:** proofread and make local corrections.
   - **Moderate:** improve flow while retaining structure and expression.
   - **Substantial:** reorganize or rewrite as requested, identifying material changes.
   Do not silently escalate intensity.
4. **Preserve the author's fingerprint.** Keep unusual but meaningful wording, metaphors, imagery, emphasis and rhythm. Do not replace distinctive language with generic professional or assistant-like phrasing merely because it sounds smoother. Do not automatically split long sentences, remove fragments or eliminate repetition; first judge whether the choice is intentional and effective. Preserve productive tension and rhetorical force. If a passage is difficult but appears deliberate, retain it and offer a separate clarity suggestion. Never infer AI authorship from abstract vocabulary, polished grammar or unusual metaphor.
5. **Distinguish change types.** A correction fixes an identifiable error; clarification explains an existing relationship; compression removes material; rewriting changes wording/structure/rhetoric; a suggestion introduces a new possibility. Do not present compression or rewriting as neutral correction.
6. **Edit to the brief.** Make changes justified by mode and intensity. Preserve perspective, intent, register and certainty. Do not add claims, examples, technical detail or emotional interpretation unsupported by the source.
7. **Run the genericity check.** Ask: could this revised passage belong to almost anyone? Have distinctive expressions become stock phrases? Is it smoother but less precise or forceful? Has a specific thought become a conventional but different argument? Restore distinctive expression or explain the trade-off.
8. **Return transparently.** Provide the revised text and briefly state intensity and meaningful edits. Keep a substantial alternative separate from the faithful edit so the user can compare.

## Stop or narrow

Ask before changing intended meaning, removing qualifications, resolving factual conflict, changing the author's position or converting deliberate expression into another register. Omit unsupported claims or label them suggestions. If requested voice conflicts with purpose, explain the tension rather than overriding the user.

## Verification

Check every fact, figure, name, quotation, condition and certainty marker; preserve the central idea and reasoning progression; ensure no claim is strengthened or qualification lost; retain intentional complexity and distinctive expression; match requested intensity; and identify any substantial rewrite, compression or new suggestion. The test is whether the writing is more effective while remaining faithful to its author.
