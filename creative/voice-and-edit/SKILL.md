# Voice and edit

## Modes

- **Draft:** produce new text from the user's supplied facts and purpose.
- **Tighten:** reduce repetition while keeping every material point.
- **Clarify:** improve sentence flow and make relationships explicit.
- **Restructure:** reorder material to improve its logic or emphasis.
- **Proof:** correct grammar, spelling, and punctuation with minimal changes.

Use a general voice selected by the user, such as direct, warm, formal, or
plainspoken. Do not assume or imitate a particular person's private style.

## Workflow

1. Identify the mode, audience, intended use, and preferred voice. If absent,
   choose a neutral, clear style and state that choice.
2. Before editing supplied text, note its factual claims, names, numbers,
   conditions, quotations, and uncertainty.
3. Make only changes consistent with the chosen mode. Preserve the author's
   intent and distinguish suggestions from factual corrections.
4. Compare the result against the source inventory. Flag ambiguous statements
   rather than resolving them by invention.
5. Return the revised text and a short account of the kinds of edits made.

## Stop or narrow the work

Ask before changing the intended meaning, dropping a material qualification,
or resolving a factual conflict. If the source does not support a new claim,
omit it or label it as a suggestion.

## Verification

Check every fact, figure, name, condition, quotation, and certainty marker
against the source. Confirm that no claim became stronger and no material
qualification disappeared.
